# Deep-dive audit — 2026-09-18/19 (M283)

Three arms, graded against a rubric fixed **before any number landed**:
measured against ourselves, against the independent solver, and against
139,508 real played hands. The rubric, its refusal conditions and the
"what each arm cannot settle" table are reproduced in full below, because
every grade this project has granted and later withdrawn (M167, M168,
M177, M180, M260's turn) was granted against a bar chosen after seeing
the data.

**Headline: the engine is accurate and honest, and it is SLOW on first
ask at a table size and depth nobody warmed.** 1,200 of 1,200 real
decisions answered with zero defects, every street inside its own
equilibrium bar — and a player sitting 8-handed at 147bb waits **211
seconds** for their first answer.

---

## The unit: usability at the table

Not "how close to GTO". A player has this open mid-hand and needs a
decision. That decomposes into four things that fail independently.

| axis | question | grade |
|---|---|---|
| **U1** | can it answer? | **A** |
| **U2** | in time? | **C overall, F at 7- and 8-handed** |
| **U3** | right where the money is? | **B on what can be graded; the flop and turn are UNGRADED** |
| **U4** | does it say when not to trust it? | ~~B~~ **D** — corrected by M285, see F59 |

**Exposure, against the hand store**: 7- and 8-handed are **7,745 real
hands = 6.82%**, and only **14.8%** of those sit in the single warmed
bucket. So **~5.81% of all real hands hit a cold 7/8-max preflop solve** —
higher exposure than every disclosed defect in this product except
multiway irreproducibility.

**Warm is fine; first-ask is the whole problem.**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**No comparison is drawn against the 2026-09-08 audit's latency**, and

anything — which is what the previous audit's own **R3** said, and R3's
instrument (`bench/reference_units.py`) was not used here either.

## U3 — accuracy

### Against ourselves: every street inside its own bar

Exploitability needs no reference and has no shared blind spot (M211).
12 spots per street, drawn off the tree, solved exactly as production
solves them. Zero dropped.

| street | n | median | p90 | worst | bar 1.0% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| flop | 12 | **0.368%** | 0.426% | 0.561% | PASS |
| turn | 12 | **0.537%** | 0.655% | 0.733% | PASS |
| river | 12 | **0.095%** | 0.110% | 0.143% | PASS |

The turn's 0.537% replicates **M278's 0.559%** and **M233's 0.562%** on
fresh spots — three independent studies agreeing, which is the bar M166
failed.

**This settles that we solve our own model well, and nothing else.**
M233 measured the two coming apart directly: at 1,000 iterations the
engine sits 0.083% from its own equilibrium while its distance from an
independent reference GROWS. A good figure here restores no withdrawn
grade.

### Against the independent solver: less is gradeable than the record implies

| street | node type | status |
|---|---|---|
| river | opening | **A** — 0.41% of pot (M260), reference gated at 0.39% |
| river | facing a bet | **B** — 0.88% of pot (M260) |
| turn | both | **UNGRADED** — A14/M277 withdrew these; the references measure 1.87–3.91% of pot against 0.33–0.49% reported, and converging 7.2x tighter moves the dumped strategy 3.91 → 3.81% |
| flop | both | **UNGRADED — see F57** |

**F57 — the flop's external figures have never passed the gate that
withdrew the turn's.** Severity: **medium**.

A13/M276 established that the solver's reported exploitability stops
describing the strategy it dumps as chance levels deepen: a river
reference (no chance node) agrees at 1.46x, a turn reference (one) fails,
and a four-bet **flop** reference (two) measured **1.46% of pot against a
reported 0.045%** — refused. A flop reference is the deepest case and the
one never checked.

It *cannot* be checked off the dumps we hold: every flop reference in the
bank was written with `set_dump_rounds 1`, so the file contains the flop
and nothing else. Walking it would value a turn leaf at raw equity while
the strategy being scored was optimised against turn and river play the
solver ran and did not serialise — M257's F62/F67, which is the
difference between an 8.4x FAIL and a 2.5x PASS on one spot.

Gating properly needs three-round dumps: ~3.5 GB and ~8–11 minutes of
solve each at this SPR, one at a time (a resident dump takes ~4.5x its
file size in RAM). A single-spot pilot was started for this audit and
**did not complete** - the session ended before the solver wrote its dump,
so no figure exists. It is queued as **R1**, not reported as run.

**What is at stake**: M260's flop grades, and `FLOP_UNDER_FOLD_NOTE`,
which is shipped user-facing copy firing on 0.58% of real decisions.

### F58 — the pinning tests pin the copy to the constant, and the constant to nothing

Severity: **medium**, and it fired **twice in the last two milestones**.

There are 18 `test_*_quotes_its_own_measurement` tests. Every one asserts
that the sentence a player reads contains the number a constant holds.
**None asserts that the constant still matches a re-run of the study it
came from.**

- **M281** shipped a setting that improved a disclosed defect and made
  its own disclosure false — the copy said 98% where the truth was 66%.
- **M282** then found the replacement figure was *also* wrong (0.6559
  against a population truth of 0.8081), because the study behind it was
  a hand-written list of 14 paths rather than the 84-node population the
  gate fires on.

Both passed the full suite while being wrong, because nothing in the
suite can tell a stale constant from a fresh one.

**U4 was published as B, and that grade was wrong.** The rubric caps U4
at D for "a warning quoting a number contradicted by a current
measurement", and at the time of this audit two did:

- `MULTIWAY_BET_NOTE` told every multiway player "47% against 19% ...
  about two and a half times as often". M269 had measured **41%** on the
  same 579 decisions and shipped the change; the note was never updated.
- `SIZING_CAVEAT_REASON` still said trash continues "98% of the time
  (measured over 22 spots)" - M251's figure - after M281 and M282 had
  both corrected the same number in the two-live warning. Its test
  asserted the literal "98%", so a correction would have failed the
  build.

Both were found by M285 building the disclosure registry this audit
recommended as R3 - the audit graded U4 without doing the one mechanical
check that would have found them. Both are corrected in M285.

### F60 — a NEARLY uniform row is served as "high" confidence

Severity: **high**. Found by R5 (M287).

At a six-handed node where everyone folds to the big blind facing an
under-the-gun open, the big blind's row is close to uniform over its four
actions **for every hand**. For example, 72o reads fold 0.28 / call 0.28 /
raise 0.22 / all-in 0.22, and KQo reads almost the same. It came back with
`trained: true` and `solver_confidence: "high"`.

- **Why no signal fired.** The prior check, `_row_is_the_prior`,
  requires EXACT uniformity (F43/M149). A row that has been visited and
  drifted a few points from the prior passes it, and so does M150's
  trainer gate.
- **What M287 changed.** The widened two-live warning now covers this
  node, so confidence reads "low" there.
- **What it did not change.** The row itself is still near-uniform.

A NEAR-prior test, or a row-entropy gate, needs its own threshold study
before it can be trusted: near-indifference is also a real answer (M149's
reason for exactness). That is **R8**.

### F61 — the multiway preflop FOLD call depends on the solver's seed

Severity: **high**. Found by R8 (M288).

Measured on the shipped 6-max 100bb solve, re-solved at three fresh
traversal seeds with equity held fixed, and weighted by how often each
node occurs in the hand store's real six-handed hands:

- **37% of real preflop decisions** sit at nodes where the average hand's
  **fold probability** moves by more than 0.10 between seeds.
- **Nodes facing an open move most:** 0.15–0.20.
- **First-in opens are stable:** under the gun 0.023, the next seats
  0.06–0.09.

`SIZING_CAVEAT_REASON` tells players the fold-vs-play call is "sounder"
than the sizing split. It is sounder at a first-in open, and not
reliably so once a player faces a raise. M163 (F46) measured the same
shape on the multiway flop, which is disclosed; the preflop cell is not.
That is **R9**.

did not survive its own instrument. That is the healthy shape.

**The one thing moving the wrong way is first-ask latency**, and it is a
direct consequence of the coverage work: three new table sizes shipped

## The rubric, as fixed before the run

### What each arm can and cannot settle

| arm | settles | cannot settle |
|---|---|---|
| against ourselves | whether we solve OUR model well, and whether the answer is reproducible | whether the model is the game (M233 showed these come apart) |
| against the independent solver | whether our model matches a different one, where its dump passes the precision gate | the turn (no valid reference), and anything it and we both get wrong |
| against real hands | coverage, latency at real stacks, defect count, how often each warning is met | correctness — a real hand's action is one player's choice, not the right answer |

### Grade thresholds

- **U1** A >= 99% of real decisions answered, B >= 95%, C >= 90%, D >= 75%.
- **U2** A p90 < 2s and max < 5s in every cell; B p90 < 5s and max < 10s;
  C p90 < 10s; D p90 < 30s; F worse, or a cell that cannot be served.
  Cold first-ask reported separately, never averaged in.
- **U3** A < 0.5% of pot, B < 1.0%, C < 2.0%, D < 4.0%. **A cell with no
  instrument that passes its own control is UNGRADED, not given the
  benefit of the doubt.**
- **U4** A all figures current, every warning fires where measured, every
  exposure known; B one stale figure or one unmeasured exposure; **capped
  at D if any warning UNDERSTATES its defect** (M232).
