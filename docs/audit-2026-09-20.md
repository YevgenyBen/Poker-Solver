# Deep-dive audit, 2026-09-20 — is this usable at the table?

Three arms, the same rubric as 2026-09-18 so the grades compare: against
**ourselves** (exploitability, no reference), against the **independent
solver** (41 fresh river references, each gated by its own control), and
against **real hands** (1,200 decisions replayed at real stacks, timed in
drift-normalised units).

Every long job ran under `bench/memory_guard.py`; peak memory and lowest
free memory are recorded per run. Nothing was killed.

| | | |
|---|---|---|

| **U2** | in time? | **C overall, F on a cold first ask** — p50 0.062s, p90 5.75s, 133 of 1,200 over five seconds; an unwarmed 8-max first ask still reaches 209s |
| **U3** | is it right? | **river A opening / B facing a bet. Flop and turn UNGRADED** — no reference passes its own control there |

## U2 — latency: C overall, and the cold first ask is worse than it was

| all 1,200 decisions | 0.062s | 5.75s | 209.0s | **133 (11.1%)** |
| preflop (n=923) | 0.013s | 5.50s | — | 97 |
| postflop (n=277) | 2.11s | 5.67s | — | 36 |

**By table size**, which is where it separates:

| size | n | p50 | p90 | max | over 5s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3-max | 47 | 2.02 | 2.38 | 7.5 | 1 |
| 4-max | 121 | 0.03 | 2.43 | 8.6 | 3 |

| 9-max | 11 | 0.00 | 1.72 | 2.6 | 0 |

**The benchmark measures an unwarmed server, deliberately** — `TestClient`
runs no lifespan, so M284's disk warmer never fills. That is the same
condition the 2026-09-18 audit measured, which is what makes the two
comparable; it is NOT what a running server serves after its warmers
finish (7-max 0.30s, 8-max 1.02s once stored, M284).

**Two things moved since the last audit, in opposite directions:**
- **M284's disk tier** turns a stored 7-/8-max bucket's first ask into a
  file read.
- **M290's six-handed ensemble made a COLD six-handed bucket 8.5x more
  expensive** (~20s → ~170s), in exchange for a fold call that moves
  half as much with the solver's seed. 96.2% of real six-handed hands
  sit at a depth the warmers cover; the rest pay that.

**Warm cost by live count** (M291, ten cold decisions per cell, quiet
machine, bar = 19.0 units): the slowest cell in the product is the
**three-way** pot — flop 24.6 units (6.49s) and turn 21.0 (5.62s),
carrying 22% and 15% of their street's real decisions. Halving that
budget was measured and refused: 1.8x faster and separably worse against
real outside play (−0.0187, −2.78σ). **F62 stands.**

## U3 — accuracy: river graded, flop and turn UNGRADED

### Against the independent solver — 41 fresh river references

Every reference passed `bench.dump_control` (reported exploitability
0.263–0.486% of pot, 41 of 41 controls ok, 4.3s a solve). Heroes drawn by
range weight (M260's R5). Rows the reference reaches under 5% of the time
are dropped; regret is graded only where our row sits on its support.

| cell | n | regret | median | net of the reference's own slack | grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **opening** | 123 | **0.325% of pot** | 0.156% | 0.106% | **A** |
| **facing a bet** | 180 | **0.985%** | 0.347% | 0.532% | **B** |

**This replicates M260 on fresh boards** — it published 0.41% opening (A)
and 0.88% facing (B) — which is the bar M166 failed and M189 passed.

**Priced in chips (M296, R6), the same rows:**

| cell | per 100 such decisions | net of the reference's slack | median | worst |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| opening | 3.01 bb | 1.06 bb | 0.013 bb | 0.24 bb |
| **facing a bet** | **11.05 bb** | **6.50 bb** | 0.031 bb | 5.48 bb |

Weighted by how often a player meets them (heads-up river is 15.8% of
real postflop decisions), the street contributes **0.87 bb per 100
postflop decisions**, 0.44 net. **It is tail-shaped**: facing a bet, the
mean is 3.6x the median, so the average is not the typical case.

Frequency distance is worse than regret, as it has been since M237: TVD
0.168 opening and 0.185 facing, both C. The two disagree because the
frequency gaps sit where the actions are worth nearly the same.

**One cell carries a caveat**: the single-raised opening cell remapped
36% of its mass between the two bet menus, so its frequency figure is
partly a menu artifact. Its regret (0.365%, A) is not.

### Flop and turn: still no instrument that passes its own control

- **The turn's references cannot be trusted** (M276/A13): a dump's
  strategy measures 1.87–3.91% of pot when the solver reports
  0.33–0.49%, and converging harder does not close it.
- **The flop needs three-round dumps**, which this machine cannot
  serialise — R1 died holding 23.8 GB of 31.7 GB.

Both stay **UNGRADED**. That is the same verdict as 2026-09-18, and it
is deliberate: a cell with no valid instrument is not given the benefit
of the doubt.

### Against ourselves — exploitability at the shipped settings

24 real decisions per street, solved exactly as the product solves them.
**This says we solve our own model well; it says nothing about whether
the model is the game** (M233 measured those coming apart), so it grades
nothing.

| street | p50 | p90 | worst | p50 in bb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| flop | 1.230% | 4.078% | 6.107% | **0.061 bb** |
| turn | 0.829% | 4.782% | 6.223% | **0.087 bb** |
| river | 0.107% | 0.479% | 1.351% | **0.020 bb** |

**The tail is a small-pot artifact and the audit says so rather than
quoting it**: correlation between pot size and percentage is **−0.68**,
and every worst case is a limped 2bb pot. In chips the figure barely
moves with the pot. Restricted to pots of 5bb or more — the population
earlier audits measured:

| street | p50 | p90 | worst |
|---|---|---|---|
| flop | 0.882% | 1.471% | 1.942% |
| turn | 0.686% | 1.312% | 2.402% |
| river | 0.106% | 0.405% | 0.526% |

Consistent with M212's 1.10 / 0.86 / 1.02% and M233/M278's turn at
~0.56%, on a population none of them used.

|---|---|
| discount bets with an open-ended straight draw | **reversed** (−0.0748, 1.49σ, n=30) |
| fold weak hands facing a bet more often | same direction, **1.40σ** (n=13) — under its own bar |

It now names no hand type, because none predicts the error, and leads
with the shape: mean 0.1026, worst 0.9037, the worst row betting 0.0716

**Why D and not C.** Eleven figures of unknown currency is what the
rubric's D describes — a player cannot tell which quoted numbers still
hold. No warning is known to understate its defect, which is the only
thing that would cap it lower.

## Direction — is this moving the right way?

Only figures measured the same way are compared.

| quantity | 2026-08-23 | 2026-09-08 | 2026-09-18 | **2026-09-20** | direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| real decisions replayed | live sim | 1,730 | 1,200 | 1,200 | — |
| product defects | 4 findings | 0 | 0 | **0** | holding |
| decisions answered | — | 97–98.5% | 100% | **100%** | holding |
| table-size coverage | — | — | 99.63% | **99.63%** | holding |
| river opening, regret vs the reference | — | — | 0.41% (A) | **0.325% (A)** | replicates |
| river facing a bet | — | — | 0.88% (B) | **0.985% (B)** | replicates |
| turn / flop accuracy | — | — | UNGRADED | **UNGRADED** | unchanged |
| disclosures not current | — | — | 12 of 23 | **11 of 26** | slightly better |
| disclosures re-derivable | none | none | 3 | **7** | better |
| decisions over five seconds | — | 1 of 1,730 | not comparable | **133 of 1,200** | **worse, and now measured** |

**The one number that looks much worse is the one that was not being
measured before.** The 2026-09-08 audit reported a single breach of five
seconds in 1,730 decisions; it drew stacks from the prewarm grid, so it
never met a cold bucket. This run draws real stacks: 11% of decisions
exceed five seconds on an unwarmed server, nearly all of them first asks
at a depth no warmer has reached yet.

**Genuine progress this window**: the latency instrument itself (M286),
a disk tier that makes a stored bucket a file read (M284), every
disclosure sourced or the build fails (M285), two-live silence measured
and found unearned (M287), the preflop fold call's seed dependence found
and disclosed (M289) then halved at six-handed (M290), and the
most-met warning in the product corrected (M292).

**The pattern holds**: of eight milestones in this window, five either
withdrew a claim or disclosed a defect, and three shipped a fix whose
cost was measured rather than assumed.
