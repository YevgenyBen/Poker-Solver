# Deep test — 2026-09-13 (M257)

A long measurement campaign against the independent solver, aimed at the
questions the last audit left open and the one it called UNKNOWN.

Three things were queued, all reading one shared bank of converged
two-round reference dumps:

- **what our postflop advice COSTS**, priced with the reference's own EV
  rather than ours — the measurement M237 and M244 each failed to make;
- **equity realisation** across SPR, board texture and range width,
  which M256 measured on a single spot;
- **the river**, if a three-round dump turns out to be affordable.

The instrument findings came first and are reported first, because two of
them invalidate work that was already on disk.

---

## F55 — reference cost is governed by SPR, not by range width

Every cost estimate in this project has been expressed in range width:
M112's near-quadratic scaling in pool size, M242's 96 minutes at cap 60,
M234's "at cap 140 it does not run at all". Width is real and on a deep
stack it is the second-order term.

| line | SPR | pool | solve | dump |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| four-bet | 2.53 | 107 | **19s** | 7 MB |
| three-bet | 6.17 | 143 | 168–289s | 24 MB |
| single-raised | 19.50 | 136 | **1,226s** | 46 MB |
| single-raised | 19.50 | 315 | 1,892s | 98 MB |

**Near-identical pool, 65× the cost.** Going from 136 combos to 315 at
the same SPR costs 1.5×; going from SPR 2.5 to 19.5 at the same width
costs 65×. A deeper stack is a deeper betting tree, and the tree is what
the solver enumerates.

The practical consequence was immediate: this campaign was planned at
36 spots and would have taken **nineteen hours**, not the ten available.
It was re-scoped on the first measurement rather than on the estimate.

## F56 — the solver fails under load, and says something that looks like data

**Severity: high. It had already put three fabricated rows into an
index.**

The failure mode is not a crash or an error code. The binary prints an
exploitability line carrying a nonsense figure and writes no dump:

    exploitability   318.0%   296.5%   118.9%      dump 0 bytes

The wrapper driving it checked that an exploitability line existed —
necessary, and not sufficient. All three were recorded as solved spots.

**The trigger is contention, not the spot.** `Kd7c2h_three_bet_c12`
returned 118.9% with no dump while other work shared the machine, and
**0.392% with a 23 MB dump when run alone** — same parameters, same
board, same ranges. So the reference's own convergence is not
reproducible under load, which is M240's drift rule applying to the
instrument rather than to a stopwatch.

**Fixed in `bench/reference_solver.py`**, which refuses a run with no
exploitability line, an implausible one, or a missing/empty dump, and
deletes stale output first — a previous run's dump left in place turns a
failed solve into a successful-looking one carrying a different spot's
data. Eight tests, each reproducing a failure that happened.

**Two guards, not one, and that is the load-bearing decision.** The bank
also filters at READ time, because it is built by a long-running job and
no study should depend on that job having been the fixed version. That
is what caught the rows the already-running process kept writing after
the file was patched.

**A third consequence, acted on**: failed rows were marked *done*, so
leaving them in place would have silently excluded those spots from the
campaign forever. They were purged so they would be retried.

## F57 — the river is still closed

M255 costed a three-round dump at **3.98 GB and 207s** at SPR 9.5, and
F55 suggested that might be an SPR fact rather than a three-round fact.
A four-bet pot at SPR 2.5 dumps 7 MB at two rounds; three rounds should
have been a few hundred megabytes.

The probe produced **no dump at all**, under contention. Re-run on a
quiet machine it succeeded, and **F55's SPR law governs dump size too**:

| | two rounds | three rounds |
|---|---|---|
| dump | 6.2 MB | **632.7 MB** (102×) |
| solve | ~36s | **64s** |
| exploitability | 0.256% | **0.256%** |

M255 measured **3.98 GB at SPR 9.5**; the same three rounds at SPR 2.53
cost **633 MB**. So the river is not closed everywhere — it is closed at
depth and **open in the shallow corner**, which is the same corner F55
made cheap to solve.

Exploitability is identical across dump depths, confirming M255's point
that how much of a solve is serialised cannot change the solve.

**This matters beyond affordability**: the spot re-solved is
`Kd7c2h_four_bet_c12`, one of the two that FAIL the external control. If
the leaf is what breaks it (F61), a three-round dump should collapse its
slack from 0.711 bb toward the 0.085 bb its exploitability implies. That
test is the campaign's last open question.

## How the pricing result will be read — fixed before the data exists

M253's lesson was that a pre-registered rule protects the reading of the
data and does nothing for an unexamined assumption in the method. Both
are written down here, in advance.

**The flop is the control, and it can void the result.** Six external
studies put this engine close to the reference on the flop (M221 median
gap 0.0099; M242 facing a bet 1.67 sigma, median +0.0002) and 0.39 away
on the turn. So:

| flop prices at | turn prices at | reading |
|---|---|---|
| ~0 | materially above 0, 2 sigma | the turn's gap has a price, and this is it |
| ~0 | ~0 | **the turn's gap is cheap** — M237 suspected it and could not establish it |
| materially above 0 | anything | **nothing is concluded**: the instrument or the mapping is wrong, because the flop is the cell we have most evidence is sound |

**The assumption being swept is the ACTION MAPPING.** Our bet sizes and
the reference's differ, so our row has to be remapped onto theirs, and a
figure that depends on how much mass had to move is not a figure. Every
row carries its remapped mass. If the mean exceeds 0.5, the result is
reported as mapping-dependent rather than as a cost.

**And width.** The bank is built at cap 12 and cap 18 because the
reference cannot be given the turn's shipped 140-class range (F55). M232
measured the turn's FREQUENCY gap doubling between cap 25 and cap 140.
Any figure that moves between the two caps by more than its own effect
size is reported as width-sensitive, not as a result — and every figure
here is at a width the product does not run, which is stated wherever it
is quoted rather than once in a footnote.

## The control that decides whether ANY of this is publishable

Written before the number exists, like the reading rule below.

After F59 the walk still shows the **reference's own** per-hand slack at
0.77 bb median on a 33 bb pot (2.3%) at a flop opening, and 4.62 bb
(14%) facing a bet, where a solve reported at 0.19–0.49% of pot suggests
~0.1 bb. Two readings fit:

1. the reported figure is a range-weighted AVERAGE and per-hand slack
   varies widely around it — in which case the walk is right;
2. the walk is still wrong.

**These are separable without a new solve.** The solver's own reported
exploitability should be reproduced by the range-weighted mean of the
per-hand slack this walk computes. `m257_selfcheck.py` computes it over
every hand in one spot's range.

| ratio, ours over reported | reading |
|---|---|
| 0.5–4× | the walk is **sound**; the spread is real, and what follows is a RESOLUTION question |
| over ~10× | the walk is **still wrong**; nothing measured here is publishable |

The 0.5–4× band rather than ≈1 is deliberate: the solver's figure sums
or averages over BOTH players and this computes one, so a factor of two
in either direction is definitional rather than evidence.

**If the walk is sound, the finding is about resolution, and the
pre-registered criterion from F58 already decides it**: a cell whose
reference slack is the size of its regret cannot be resolved. On the
rows measured so far that means the **flop opening cell is measurable
and facing a bet is NOT** — the reference's own slack there (4.62 bb)
exceeds our regret (3.23 bb). That would make M257 the **fourth**
failed attempt to price this engine's postflop advice — after M237 and
M244 — and the first to say precisely why and to quantify the bar a
reference must clear to do it.

## F58 — the pricing metric measured two things at once

**Severity: high, and it invalidates the metric rather than the
machinery.** Found from a number that cannot happen.

`price_row` scores our row as `EV(reference row) − EV(our row)`, both
computed inside the reference's game with the opponent and every
continuation held at the reference's strategy. The design rests on one
assumption nobody wrote down: **that the reference's row is the best
answer available at that node.**

Over a whole range it very nearly is — these solves converge to
0.19–0.49% of pot. But that is an AVERAGE over the range, and a single
hand's row can sit far from its own best response. The campaign priced
`8d8c` in a four-bet pot at **−2.36 bb with 0.2% of mass remapped**: our
row beating the reference's, inside the reference's own game, one step
from a node where nothing else differs. A converged row makes that
impossible.

So the published quantity was **our error minus the instrument's own
per-hand slack**, and a negative value says nothing about us.

**The fix is a quantity that cannot be negative.**

    regret = max_a Q(a) − EV(our row)

`Walk.regret_of_row` returns it, and returns the reference's own slack
at the same node beside it. Regret depends on the reference only through
the game it defines, never through how well it solved one hand.
The slack measures the instrument: **a cell whose reference slack is the
size of its regret is a cell this dump cannot resolve**, and is reported
as such rather than published.

**This is M253's lesson a second time, and it landed on the method.**
The reading rule below was pre-registered before the run and it is
sound; what it could not protect was its own input assumption. M253
swept a realisation factor and found the verdict flipping at 0.87. Here
the unexamined input was "the reference's row for this hand is
optimal" — and an impossible number, not the rule, is what caught it.

Both metrics are reported from here on, because **the difference between
them is the instrument's error.**

## F59 — the walk priced against a range the reference never played

**Severity: highest here. It withdraws a published figure.**

`Walk.initial_reach` gave every unblocked villain combo weight **1.0**.
The solver is handed a weighted range, and a real params file from this
very bank reads:

    set_range_ip JJ:0.6592,AA:0.2375,AKo:0.1566,QQ:0.1352,TT:0.1151,
                 AKs:0.07527,KK:0.01993,99:0.007941,AQs:0.005656,
                 AQo:0.002679,KQo:0.001581,88:0.000908

**Weights spanning 700×, flattened to one.** The reference's strategy is
an equilibrium against *those* weights, so every EV computed here was
hero's EV against an opponent the reference never played — and the
reference's own rows are then scored on a game they were not solving.

**How it surfaced, and only because F58 had just been built**: with
regret measured against the best action, the *reference's own* per-hand
slack came out at **2.1–5.0 bb on a 33 bb pot**. A solve converged to
0.19–0.49% of pot should sit near 0.1 bb. Two orders of magnitude is not
a convergence story, it is a wrong question — and one hand (`8d8c`) came
back with our row beating the reference's inside the reference's own
game, which a converged row cannot allow.

**M256's four controls could not see this, and that is the finding.**
The exact pairwise conservation control — the one that caught hero's own
cards in the runout, and took the walker's error from 0.80% to 0.00% —
holds for **any** reach weights, provided both sides use the same ones.
It passed at 0.00% while the range was flat. **M219's dead guard in a
new place: an assertion that cannot distinguish the two things it
compares.**

**Consequences.**

- **M256's realisation figures are withdrawn** — median 0.2858 out of
  position and 1.1652 in position were measured this way. `CLAUDE.md`
  carried them and now carries the withdrawal.
- Every pricing row measured before this was purged rather than
  corrected.
- **The dump does not carry the ranges.** Its top level is only
  `actions`, `childrens`, `node_type`, `player`, `strategy`. Any walk
  over a dump must read the params file written beside it —
  `parse_params_ranges` does, and `initial_reach(board, weights)` takes
  the result.

**Three instrument defects in one campaign (F56, F58, F59), each found
by a number that could not be true rather than by a test.** The pattern
worth carrying: the controls that caught M256's bugs were all *internal
consistency* checks, and internal consistency is exactly what a wrong
input preserves.

## F60 — stopping a background task did not stop the job

Twice in this campaign a study was patched and relaunched while the
PREVIOUS run was still alive, and both wrote the same JSON index with
different schemas. The rows had to be purged both times.

The cause is that stopping a background task killed the shell and left
its Python child running — confirmed by listing processes: a run started
at 23:57 was still writing at 00:41, two "stops" later, alongside the
run that replaced it.

**Check the PROCESS, not the task.** `Get-CimInstance Win32_Process`
with the command line shows what is really running; stop by PID, and
confirm only the intended jobs survive before relaunching. This is F56's
lesson pointed at my own harness rather than at the solver: a job that
was believed dead kept producing output that looked like data.

**And a third collision, from the fix rather than the bug.** Jobs were
then chained by waiting for a process NAME to be absent — which races
when the job has not started yet. The moment the bank exited, a waiter
checked for the river test, did not find it because it was still
launching, and started a competing study against the solver run it was
supposed to follow.

**Wait on a specific PID, or chain sequentially in one command.** A
name-absence check cannot distinguish "finished" from "not started",
which is M219's dead guard once more: an assertion that cannot tell
apart the two states it is asked about.

## F61 — the control failed ON ONE SPOT, and I over-read it (CORRECTED)

The self-check ran on `Kd7c2h_four_bet_c12` (pool 95, pot 33 bb,
reported exploitability **0.256%**, solver target 0.5%, so converged).
Range-weighted mean of the reference's own per-hand slack:

| | |
|---|---|
| our figure, 26 hands | **0.711 bb = 2.155% of pot** |
| the solver's own | **0.256% of pot** (≈ 0.085 bb) |
| **ratio** | **8.4×** |
| median slack | 0.727 bb |
| worst | 1.733 bb |
| negative slacks | **0** — regret is non-negative as F58 built it |

**The pre-registered rule had a gap and the number landed in it** — 0.5–4×
was "sound", over 10× was "wrong", and 8.4 is neither. Recorded as a
defect in the rule rather than resolved by widening a band after the
fact.

**An argument that does not need the band settles it.** At an
ε-equilibrium no single-node deviation can gain more than ε, and hero's
regret at the root IS such a deviation — strictly weaker than the full
best response exploitability measures. So its range-weighted mean must
be **bounded by** the reported figure. At 9.6× it is not. **The walk
overstates, and the excess is the instrument's.**

### The correction, and it reverses the conclusion above

**F61 as first written said "the walk overstates". That is false as a
general claim and I published it before checking a second spot.**

The self-check picked the cheapest spot in the bank — smallest pool —
and that spot is an outlier. Across all six four-bet spots, at the flop
opening:

| spot | reference slack, median | as % of pot | reported exploitability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Th9c8d | 0.00001 | 0.000% | 0.101% |
| 9d9s4c | 0.00012 | 0.000% | 0.298% |
| 8h6h2s | 0.00110 | 0.003% | 0.191% |
| 5c4d2h | 0.00231 | 0.007% | 0.129% |
| **Ac7d2h** | **0.32962** | **0.999%** | 0.431% |
| **Kd7c2h** | **0.76526** | **2.319%** | 0.256% |

**Four of six sit far BELOW the reported exploitability, exactly as the
ε-bound requires.** Two violate it — and the self-check ran on one of
them. Convergence does not explain the split: `Kd7c2h` has the
second-lowest reported exploitability and the worst slack.

So the walk is sound on most spots and defective on some, which is a
different and much more useful finding than "the walk overstates".

**The two offenders are both high-card boards** (A-high, K-high) and the
four clean ones are low or middling. That is a hypothesis on **n=6**,
which is exactly the sample size M166 asserted a split from and M167
withdrew — recorded as a lead, not a finding.

**What this cost**: a published claim that had to be corrected within
the hour, because one spot was treated as the instrument. The rule
this campaign keeps re-learning is M168's — measure the thing you intend
to generalise over.

### The original suspicion, now applying to two spots rather than all

**The leaf is the suspect, and M256 wrote the caveat nobody weighed**:
"the river valued at exact equity, which is not a bound in either
direction." A two-round dump carries the flop and the turn. The solver
*solved* the river — it just did not serialise it — so the strategy
being scored was optimised against real river play, while this walk
values a turn leaf at `equity × pot`, as if the hand checked down.
**Scoring a strategy under a continuation it never agreed to makes its
own rows look suboptimal by however wrong that continuation is**, and
that error lands on both arms, so it does not cancel.

It also predicts the shape already seen: facing a bet is worse than a
flop opening, and facing-a-bet lines are the ones that reach a river
with money behind.

**The three-round test was refused by this campaign's own guard, and I
caused that.** F56's entry says in so many words to solve on a quiet
machine, one at a time; the test was launched while the bank was still
solving, and the run came back at **1.420% exploitability with a 0-byte
dump** — F56's exact signature. The guard turned what would once have
been three fabricated rows into a clean refusal, which is the system
working. **The collision was avoidable and the rule that forbade it was
mine.** The same contention cost a bank spot
(`9d9s4c_single_raised_c12`, 0.892% and no dump, correctly not
recorded).

Re-run alone, the three-round test remains the decisive experiment and
F57 stays open until it runs.

## F62 — the leaf WAS the cause, and adding the river fixes the control

The decisive experiment, run on `Kd7c2h_four_bet_c12` — **one of the two
spots that fail the control** — with nothing changed but how much of the
solve was serialised:

| dump | mean slack | % of pot | ratio vs solver | control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| two rounds | 0.711 bb | 2.155% | **8.4×** | **FAIL** |
| **three rounds** | **0.207 bb** | **0.627%** | **2.5×** | **PASS** |

**2.5× is inside `MAX_PLAUSIBLE_RATIO` (4.0), fixed before any of this
ran.** Slack falls **3.4×**, the median falls 0.727 → 0.125, and the
worst case 1.733 → 0.593. Exploitability is identical across the two
dumps (0.256%), so the solve did not change — only what the walk could
see.

**F61's hypothesis is confirmed and its "high-card board" lead is
probably a red herring.** The variable is not the board's top card, it
is **how much the river matters on that spot**. A two-round dump values
a turn leaf at `equity × pot` — as if the hand checked down — while the
strategy being scored was optimised against river play the solver ran
and simply did not serialise. Spots where that approximation is cheap
pass the control; spots where it is expensive fail it. Both are the same
walk.

**What this settles.**

- The walk is **sound**. It reproduces an independent figure it does not
  compute, once given the tree the reference actually solved.
- **A two-round dump cannot price a decision whose river matters**, and
  it fails silently — 8.4× with no error.
- The fix is affordable exactly where F55 says: 633 MB and 64s at SPR
  2.53, against M255's 3.98 GB at SPR 9.5.

**What it costs the measurement above.** The turn's 3.16%-of-pot figure
was taken from **two-round dumps**, on the four spots that passed the
two-round control — i.e. spots where the river approximation happened to
be cheap. That is the right filter and it is not the same as being
right: **the honest next step is to re-price those cells on three-round
dumps**, which at SPR 2.53 is affordable and at SPR ≥ 5 is not.

**A residual 2.5× remains** and is not explained. It is inside the band
allowed for the solver's figure covering both players where this covers
one, so it needs no further explanation to be usable — but it is not
zero, and calling it definitional is an assumption, not a measurement.

## Findings from the measurement itself

**On the spots that pass the external control, the pre-registered rule
returns a verdict.** Four four-bet spots, SPR 2.53, cap 12, pot 33 bb,
scored as regret inside the reference's game (F58) against the
reference's real weighted ranges (F59):

| cell | n | regret median | regret mean | % of pot | sigma | ref slack | remapped |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **flop opening** (control) | 28 | 0.0082 | **0.0802** | **0.24%** | **1.16** | 0.00015 | 0.003 |
| **turn opening** | 42 | 0.0595 | **1.0412** | **3.16%** | **3.82** | 0.0883 | 0.005 |
| flop facing a bet | 24 | 2.1397 | 3.0512 | 9.25% | 6.07 | 1.1671 | 0.380 |

**The control passes**: the flop's opening decision prices at 0.24% of
pot and **is not separable from zero at 1.16 sigma**, which is what six
external studies predicted and what the pre-registered rule required
before anything else could be read.

**The turn separates at 3.82 sigma.** By the rule fixed before the data
existed, that reads: *the turn's gap has a price, and this is it.*

**It is not a mapping artifact.** Mean remapped mass is **0.005** on
both cells, against the 0.5 threshold pre-registered as the point where
a figure becomes mapping-dependent. The assumption swept turned out not
to matter here.

**It is resolvable.** Turn regret (1.0412) is **12× the reference's own
slack** (0.0883); the flop opening is 500×. Both clear F58's criterion.

### What this does NOT say

- **It is the SHALLOW regime, and that is the wrong one.** SPR 2.53,
  because F55 made deep spots 65× more expensive. M199 put **80% of real
  decisions at SPR ≥ 5, median 9.5**, and M222 measured the turn's
  frequency gap as SPR-dependent and *smallest* when shallow. So this
  prices the turn where the disagreement is mildest. Whether it grows
  with depth is **not measured**, and M168 forbids assuming it.
- **Cap 12 is not a width the product runs** (the turn ships at 140), and
  M232 measured the turn's frequency gap doubling between cap 25 and cap
  140.
- **The tail carries the mean**: 1.0412 against a median of 0.0595, a
  factor of 17. This project's usual shape (M183 — five spots of 48
  carried 79% of all loss), and it means the figure is tail-sensitive
  and n=42 is thin.
- **Excluding the two control-failing spots was decided AFTER seeing the
  data.** The criterion is principled — they violate a bound that must
  hold — but the decision was post hoc, so **this needs replication on
  fresh spots** before it is quoted anywhere user-facing. M166 is what
  skipping that looks like.

### How the DEPTH replication will be read — fixed before it runs

The bank holds `three_bet` at **SPR 6.17** and `single_raised` at **SPR
19.50**, so R7 is answerable without new solves. Written down now:

| turn regret at SPR 6.17 / 19.50 vs 3.16% at SPR 2.53 | reading |
|---|---|
| materially larger, 2 sigma | **3.16% is a FLOOR** — the shallow regime understates, as M222's SPR-dependence predicts, and the figure that matters is the deep one |
| comparable | the turn's price is roughly depth-independent, and 3.16% travels |
| materially smaller | the shallow regime OVERSTATES, and the headline above is the wrong end of the range |

**Each spot is gated individually by `bench.dump_control`** rather than
the study as a whole — that is what preserved a result from this
campaign at all, and the deep spots are the ones whose references
converge worst (0.85–0.89% against a four-bet pot's 0.10–0.43%), so more
of them may fail the gate. **A deep cell that fails the control is
reported as unresolvable, not as a small number.**

**Width comes free with it**: `three_bet` exists at cap 12 and cap 18,
so the pre-registered width sweep runs on the same rows. A figure moving
between the two caps by more than its own effect size is reported as
width-sensitive rather than as a result.

### The two things worth carrying anyway

**Facing a bet is 38× the opening decision** (3.0512 against 0.0802).
M188/M189 measured 20–27× from inside this engine's own model; this
reproduces the shape from an instrument that shares none of its
assumptions.

**And it points the opposite way to M237.** M237 priced the turn's
frequency gap at **−0.0266 bb** using our own tree, hand values and
opponent — and said in its own entry that a model error had been priced
with the model that has it. Priced inside the reference's game instead,
the same decision type costs **+1.04 bb mean / +0.059 median**. Different
sign, and two orders of magnitude on the mean. The populations and
widths differ, so this is **suggestive rather than a refutation** — but
it is the first evidence that M237's near-zero was an artifact of
self-refereeing rather than a fact about the turn.

## What this campaign actually established

**Seven findings about the instrument, and one measurement that
survived them.**

Most of this campaign was spent discovering that the apparatus was
wrong — three times, each caught by a number that could not be true
rather than by any test that existed. What came out the other side is
narrow and real.

**The headline was delivered, on the spots that pass an external
control**: priced inside the reference's own game, the turn's opening
decision costs **1.04 bb mean / 0.059 bb median, 3.16% of pot, at 3.82
sigma**, while the flop's opening decision — the pre-registered control
— is **not separable from zero**. That is the first time this project
has priced a postflop decision with something other than its own model.
M237 and M244 both failed at it; the difference here is that the tree,
the opponent and every continuation come from the reference, so the two
arms differ in exactly one decision.

**It is bounded hard by scope**, and the scope is the weak part: SPR
2.53 (the shallow regime, where M222 says the turn's disagreement is
*smallest*, while M199 puts 80% of real decisions at SPR ≥ 5), cap 12,
four boards, 42 rows, a tail-carried mean, and a spot exclusion decided
after seeing the data. **It is a result that needs replication, not a
number to put in front of a player.**

**What is now known that was not**: pricing a postflop decision from
outside is *possible*, it costs a converged per-hand reference, and the
instrument fails on some spots for reasons not yet understood — both
offenders here were high-card boards, on n=6.

## Recommendations

Five are implemented on this branch. They are listed with what they cost
and what they buy, not as a checklist.

**R1 — Guard the reference solver. DONE** (`bench/reference_solver.py`,
8 tests). It had already put three fabricated rows into an index. The
guard has since run a full bank rebuild with **zero bad rows admitted**,
and refused two contended runs that would previously have been recorded
as data.

**R2 — Scope reference campaigns by SPR, not by width. DONE**
(`CLAUDE.md`). 65× at a near-identical pool. A campaign scoped the old
way came to nineteen hours against ten available.

**R3 — Score rows by regret, never by the difference of two EVs. DONE**
(`Walk.regret_of_row`, 3 tests). The difference silently subtracts the
reference's own per-hand slack, which is how a hand came back beating
the reference inside the reference's own game.

**R4 — Never walk a dump with a uniform reach. DONE**
(`parse_params_ranges`, `initial_reach(board, weights)`, 5 tests). The
dump does not carry the ranges; the params beside it do. **This withdrew
M256's published realisation figures**, which is the cost of the fix and
