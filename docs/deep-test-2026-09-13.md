# Deep test — 2026-09-13 (M257)

A long measurement campaign against the independent solver, aimed at the
questions the last audit left open and the one it called UNKNOWN.

Three things were queued, all reading one shared bank of converged
two-round reference dumps:

- **what our postflop advice COSTS**, priced with the reference's own EV
  rather than ours — the measurement M237 and M244 each failed to make;
- **equity realisation** across SPR, board texture and range width,
  which M256 measured on a single spot;
- **the river**, if a three-round dump turns out to be affordable.

The instrument findings came first and are reported first, because two of
them invalidate work that was already on disk.

---

## F55 — reference cost is governed by SPR, not by range width

Every cost estimate in this project has been expressed in range width:
M112's near-quadratic scaling in pool size, M242's 96 minutes at cap 60,
M234's "at cap 140 it does not run at all". Width is real and on a deep
stack it is the second-order term.

| line | SPR | pool | solve | dump |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| four-bet | 2.53 | 107 | **19s** | 7 MB |
| three-bet | 6.17 | 143 | 168–289s | 24 MB |
| single-raised | 19.50 | 136 | **1,226s** | 46 MB |
| single-raised | 19.50 | 315 | 1,892s | 98 MB |

**Near-identical pool, 65× the cost.** Going from 136 combos to 315 at
the same SPR costs 1.5×; going from SPR 2.5 to 19.5 at the same width
costs 65×. A deeper stack is a deeper betting tree, and the tree is what
the solver enumerates.

The practical consequence was immediate: this campaign was planned at
36 spots and would have taken **nineteen hours**, not the ten available.
It was re-scoped on the first measurement rather than on the estimate.

## F56 — the solver fails under load, and says something that looks like data

**Severity: high. It had already put three fabricated rows into an
index.**

The failure mode is not a crash or an error code. The binary prints an
exploitability line carrying a nonsense figure and writes no dump:

    exploitability   318.0%   296.5%   118.9%      dump 0 bytes

The wrapper driving it checked that an exploitability line existed —
necessary, and not sufficient. All three were recorded as solved spots.

**The trigger is contention, not the spot.** `Kd7c2h_three_bet_c12`
returned 118.9% with no dump while other work shared the machine, and
**0.392% with a 23 MB dump when run alone** — same parameters, same
board, same ranges. So the reference's own convergence is not
reproducible under load, which is M240's drift rule applying to the
instrument rather than to a stopwatch.

**Fixed in `bench/reference_solver.py`**, which refuses a run with no
exploitability line, an implausible one, or a missing/empty dump, and
deletes stale output first — a previous run's dump left in place turns a
failed solve into a successful-looking one carrying a different spot's
data. Eight tests, each reproducing a failure that happened.

**Two guards, not one, and that is the load-bearing decision.** The bank
also filters at READ time, because it is built by a long-running job and
no study should depend on that job having been the fixed version. That
is what caught the rows the already-running process kept writing after
the file was patched.

**A third consequence, acted on**: failed rows were marked *done*, so
leaving them in place would have silently excluded those spots from the
campaign forever. They were purged so they would be retried.

## F58 — the pricing metric measured two things at once

**Severity: high, and it invalidates the metric rather than the
machinery.** Found from a number that cannot happen.

`price_row` scores our row as `EV(reference row) − EV(our row)`, both
computed inside the reference's game with the opponent and every
continuation held at the reference's strategy. The design rests on one
assumption nobody wrote down: **that the reference's row is the best
answer available at that node.**

Over a whole range it very nearly is — these solves converge to
0.19–0.49% of pot. But that is an AVERAGE over the range, and a single
hand's row can sit far from its own best response. The campaign priced
`8d8c` in a four-bet pot at **−2.36 bb with 0.2% of mass remapped**: our
row beating the reference's, inside the reference's own game, one step
from a node where nothing else differs. A converged row makes that
impossible.

So the published quantity was **our error minus the instrument's own
per-hand slack**, and a negative value says nothing about us.

**The fix is a quantity that cannot be negative.**

    regret = max_a Q(a) − EV(our row)

`Walk.regret_of_row` returns it, and returns the reference's own slack
at the same node beside it. Regret depends on the reference only through
the game it defines, never through how well it solved one hand.
The slack measures the instrument: **a cell whose reference slack is the
size of its regret is a cell this dump cannot resolve**, and is reported
as such rather than published.

**This is M253's lesson a second time, and it landed on the method.**
The reading rule below was pre-registered before the run and it is
sound; what it could not protect was its own input assumption. M253
swept a realisation factor and found the verdict flipping at 0.87. Here
the unexamined input was "the reference's row for this hand is
optimal" — and an impossible number, not the rule, is what caught it.

Both metrics are reported from here on, because **the difference between
them is the instrument's error.**

## F60 — stopping a background task did not stop the job

Twice in this campaign a study was patched and relaunched while the
PREVIOUS run was still alive, and both wrote the same JSON index with
different schemas. The rows had to be purged both times.

The cause is that stopping a background task killed the shell and left
its Python child running — confirmed by listing processes: a run started
at 23:57 was still writing at 00:41, two "stops" later, alongside the
run that replaced it.

**Check the PROCESS, not the task.** `Get-CimInstance Win32_Process`
with the command line shows what is really running; stop by PID, and
confirm only the intended jobs survive before relaunching. This is F56's
lesson pointed at my own harness rather than at the solver: a job that
was believed dead kept producing output that looked like data.

## F59 — the walk priced against a range the reference never played

**Severity: highest here. It withdraws a published figure.**

`Walk.initial_reach` gave every unblocked villain combo weight **1.0**.
The solver is handed a weighted range, and a real params file from this
very bank reads:

    set_range_ip JJ:0.6592,AA:0.2375,AKo:0.1566,QQ:0.1352,TT:0.1151,
                 AKs:0.07527,KK:0.01993,99:0.007941,AQs:0.005656,
                 AQo:0.002679,KQo:0.001581,88:0.000908

**Weights spanning 700×, flattened to one.** The reference's strategy is
an equilibrium against *those* weights, so every EV computed here was
hero's EV against an opponent the reference never played — and the
reference's own rows are then scored on a game they were not solving.

**How it surfaced, and only because F58 had just been built**: with
regret measured against the best action, the *reference's own* per-hand
slack came out at **2.1–5.0 bb on a 33 bb pot**. A solve converged to
0.19–0.49% of pot should sit near 0.1 bb. Two orders of magnitude is not
a convergence story, it is a wrong question — and one hand (`8d8c`) came
back with our row beating the reference's inside the reference's own
game, which a converged row cannot allow.

**M256's four controls could not see this, and that is the finding.**
The exact pairwise conservation control — the one that caught hero's own
cards in the runout, and took the walker's error from 0.80% to 0.00% —
holds for **any** reach weights, provided both sides use the same ones.
It passed at 0.00% while the range was flat. **M219's dead guard in a
new place: an assertion that cannot distinguish the two things it
compares.**

**Consequences.**

- **M256's realisation figures are withdrawn** — median 0.2858 out of
  position and 1.1652 in position were measured this way. `CLAUDE.md`
  carried them and now carries the withdrawal.
- Every pricing row measured before this was purged rather than
  corrected.
- **The dump does not carry the ranges.** Its top level is only
  `actions`, `childrens`, `node_type`, `player`, `strategy`. Any walk
  over a dump must read the params file written beside it —
  `parse_params_ranges` does, and `initial_reach(board, weights)` takes
  the result.

**Three instrument defects in one campaign (F56, F58, F59), each found
by a number that could not be true rather than by a test.** The pattern
worth carrying: the controls that caught M256's bugs were all *internal
consistency* checks, and internal consistency is exactly what a wrong
input preserves.

## The control that decides whether ANY of this is publishable

Written before the number exists, like the reading rule below.

After F59 the walk still shows the **reference's own** per-hand slack at
0.77 bb median on a 33 bb pot (2.3%) at a flop opening, and 4.62 bb
(14%) facing a bet, where a solve reported at 0.19–0.49% of pot suggests
~0.1 bb. Two readings fit:

1. the reported figure is a range-weighted AVERAGE and per-hand slack
   varies widely around it — in which case the walk is right;
2. the walk is still wrong.

**These are separable without a new solve.** The solver's own reported
exploitability should be reproduced by the range-weighted mean of the
per-hand slack this walk computes. `m257_selfcheck.py` computes it over
every hand in one spot's range.

| ratio, ours over reported | reading |
|---|---|
| 0.5–4× | the walk is **sound**; the spread is real, and what follows is a RESOLUTION question |
| over ~10× | the walk is **still wrong**; nothing measured here is publishable |

The 0.5–4× band rather than ≈1 is deliberate: the solver's figure sums
or averages over BOTH players and this computes one, so a factor of two
in either direction is definitional rather than evidence.

**If the walk is sound, the finding is about resolution, and the
pre-registered criterion from F58 already decides it**: a cell whose
reference slack is the size of its regret cannot be resolved. On the
rows measured so far that means the **flop opening cell is measurable
and facing a bet is NOT** — the reference's own slack there (4.62 bb)
exceeds our regret (3.23 bb). That would make M257 the **fourth**
failed attempt to price this engine's postflop advice — after M237 and
M244 — and the first to say precisely why and to quantify the bar a
reference must clear to do it.

## How the pricing result will be read — fixed before the data exists

M253's lesson was that a pre-registered rule protects the reading of the
data and does nothing for an unexamined assumption in the method. Both
are written down here, in advance.

**The flop is the control, and it can void the result.** Six external
studies put this engine close to the reference on the flop (M221 median
gap 0.0099; M242 facing a bet 1.67 sigma, median +0.0002) and 0.39 away
on the turn. So:

| flop prices at | turn prices at | reading |
|---|---|---|
| ~0 | materially above 0, 2 sigma | the turn's gap has a price, and this is it |
| ~0 | ~0 | **the turn's gap is cheap** — M237 suspected it and could not establish it |
| materially above 0 | anything | **nothing is concluded**: the instrument or the mapping is wrong, because the flop is the cell we have most evidence is sound |

**The assumption being swept is the ACTION MAPPING.** Our bet sizes and
the reference's differ, so our row has to be remapped onto theirs, and a
figure that depends on how much mass had to move is not a figure. Every
row carries its remapped mass. If the mean exceeds 0.5, the result is
reported as mapping-dependent rather than as a cost.

**And width.** The bank is built at cap 12 and cap 18 because the
reference cannot be given the turn's shipped 140-class range (F55). M232
measured the turn's FREQUENCY gap doubling between cap 25 and cap 140.
Any figure that moves between the two caps by more than its own effect
size is reported as width-sensitive, not as a result — and every figure
here is at a width the product does not run, which is stated wherever it
is quoted rather than once in a footnote.
