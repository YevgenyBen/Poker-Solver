# Wide benchmark and audit — 2026-09-08 (M252)

Fourth full audit. The first drove M27–M34, the second M58–M65, the
third (M101) M102 onward. This one is a wide benchmark of the shipped
product — 1,730 real `/advise` decisions across two independently
generated populations — asking a question no milestone here has asked:

> **How often does a player actually meet each of the defects this
> project has spent fifty milestones disclosing?**

Every accuracy study in this repository picks a spot set designed to
contain the thing being studied. That is correct for measuring a defect
and useless for ranking defects against each other. CLAUDE.md's own
thesis says weight by how often a spot occurs before quoting any
per-hand claim; nothing had ever done that across the whole disclosure
list.

Findings are ordered by what they cost a user.

---

## Method, and why there are two populations

Spots are generated by **walking the real betting tree** rather than
assembling action names, so every path is legal by construction and the
population reaches whatever depth the tree offers.

Two arms, differing only in how strongly the walk prefers raising:

| arm | raise weight | decisions | 2+ raises on the path |
|---|---|---|---|
| aggressive | 3.0 | 888 | **61.1%** |
| as it comes | 1.0 | 842 | **30.2%** |

**Neither is a real-play frequency distribution and neither claims to
be.** They bracket the behaviour: a number that holds across both is not
an artifact of the weighting, and a number that moves between them is
generator-sensitive and is reported as such.

Both arms warm the multiway preflop depths first — see F54(c).

Share of decisions whose `solver_confidence_reason` names each
disclosed defect:

| disclosure | aggressive | as it comes |
|---|---|---|
| **multiway postflop is not reproducible** (M245) | **20.05%** | **19.83%** |

**One decision in five is served with "this answer is not

`solver_confidence` is `"low"` on **21.4% / 20.3%** of decisions.

## F51 — `aggression_confidence` is a function of street and nothing else

**Severity: medium. The highest-exposure finding here.**

| | preflop | postflop |
|---|---|---|
| aggressive arm | high, 360 of 360 | **low, 528 of 528** |
| as it comes | high, 360 of 360 | **low, 482 of 482** |

Every postflop decision in 1,010 gets the same level. A client that
switches on it — a badge, a colour, a filter — shows the identical thing
on every postflop answer, which is the same as showing nothing.

**The level is not WRONG.** Aggression really is unreliable on every
postflop street, and that is measured (M140: mean error 0.1394, worst
0.8810). It is honest and uninformative at the same time.

**The information exists and is locked in prose.** Over 67 postflop
decisions the level took **one** value and the reason string took
**25 distinct forms** — `_aggression_reason` composes up to ten notes
gated on street, hand-strength band, SPR, board texture, whether hero
faces a bet, and M189's costly band. All of the per-decision signal is
there; none of it is machine-readable. A caller wanting to act on it has
to pattern-match English.

The precedent for the fix is already in this codebase: M166 added
`hand_strength_percentile` as a structured field precisely so "a caller
can act on it directly instead of parsing prose." The same argument
applies to which notes fired.

**Recommendation R1 (executed):** `advisory_notes` now carries a stable
id per fired caveat, derived from the same computation as the prose —
`_advisory_notes` returns `(id, text)` pairs and `_aggression_reason`
joins their text, so the two cannot drift, and a test asserts the join
reproduces the paragraph character for character.

**Do NOT try to make the level itself vary.** Grading it would require
claiming a postflop cell where aggression is reliable, and no such cell
has ever been certified — `CERTIFY_RELIABILITY_ON_STREETS` is empty, and
M167/M168/M177/M180 are four separate refusals to grant one. A test
pins the level's constancy so that if a street is ever certified, whoever
does it has to decide what the level should then mean.

## F52 — M251's gate covers under half of its own cell

**Severity: medium.**

M251 shipped a warning for multiway preflop pots folded down to two live
players facing a re-raise, measured at 22 of 22 nodes telling 72o-class
hands to continue. In the wild:

| arm | cell occurrences | warned |
|---|---|---|
| aggressive | 22 of 888 (2.48%) | **10** |
| as it comes | 22 of 842 (2.61%) | **4** |

The unwarned remainder sits entirely at **0 or 1 raises on the path**,
below `PREFLOP_TWO_LIVE_MIN_TO_CALL_BB`. That is the gate working as
designed — M251 measured a band of 0.2222–0.2727 in price and 9.0bb
owed, and firing outside it would be quoting a 22-node finding over
spots those 22 never covered.

**What is NOT claimed: that the unwarned ones are wrong.** Several are
weak hands never folding (85o at 0.0, Q9o at 0.0002, T4o at 0.0118) and
that looks alarming — but two live players preflop with no raise is a
blind-versus-blind spot, where playing extremely wide is correct.
**M127 made exactly this mistake once**, flagging 84o calling in the big
blind as a defect when 84o really does have the equity heads-up. The
honest position is that the 0–1 raise two-live cell is *unmeasured*.

**Recommendation R2 (not executed):** measure it, the way M251 measured
the deep cell — enumerated off the tree, priced against the equity
table. It is one study and it decides whether the gate should widen.

## F53 — the flop's five-second headroom is gone

**Severity: low, but it is the bar this project set itself.**

| arm | p50 | p90 | p99 | max | over 5s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| aggressive | 0.726 | 1.952 | 3.347 | 4.307 | **0 of 888** |
| as it comes | 0.783 | 2.239 | 3.484 | **5.613** | **1 of 842** |

By street (aggressive arm): preflop p50 0.002 / max 4.307, flop 1.481 /
3.120, turn 1.002 / 2.076, river 0.756 / 3.435.

The single breach is a **6-max flop with six players live at 100bb,
5.613s**. M234 measured the flop's worst case at 4.994s and described
the remaining headroom as 1.4% "on a machine measured drifting 1.7x";
M240 later measured that machine drifting **9.7x inside a single run**.

**Recommendation R3 (not executed):** re-measure the flop tail in
reference units (`bench.reference_units`), which is exactly the
instrument M240 built for claims like this one. A single breach on a
drifting machine is not evidence of a regression, and it is not evidence
of safety either.

## F54 — three defects in the benchmark itself *(all fixed)*

The instrument was audited alongside the product, and it was in worse
shape.

**(a) The spot generator could not reach the defects.** Every play
session in this project drew preflop action from

```python
rng.choice([[], [], ["raise"], ["fold"] * (players - 2) + ["raise"]])
```

— at most **one raise**. Preflop advice was therefore never once asked
at a 3-bet node, let alone the 4-bet where M251's defect lives.

Worse, the harness *had* a guard for that defect: it flagged trash
folding under **0.02** while facing action. The value M251 measured is
**0.0269**. A threshold set just below the defect, over a population
that could not reach it.

Fixed by `bench/spot_population.py`, which walks the real tree. The new
population puts 30–61% of decisions at depths the old one never
produced.

**(b) Streets were closed with a fixed number of actions rather than one
per live player.** Across **85 recorded sessions, 1,552 of 24,150
attempted requests (6.4%) were refused**, from two distinct causes:

| refused | cause |
|---|---|
| 1,167 flop | a preflop line assembled from action names that does not close the betting |
| 346 turn, 39 river | a flop closed with two checks regardless of live count |

The second is the interesting one. Two checks close a street only
heads-up; three-handed the betting stays open and the API correctly
refuses to deal the next card, so **the multiway turn and river spots
were dropped rather than measured** — which is why M245 had to build its
own harness to study them.

Fixed by `closing_path(live_players)`, with a test that asserts against
a real street tree that two checks do *not* close a three-handed street.
The new harness's refusal rate is **1.5–3.0%**, and what remains is
spots that genuinely cannot be asked.

**(c) `TestClient` never runs the app's lifespan, so nothing is
prewarmed.** The first run of this benchmark recorded a **72.6-second**
preflop request and five more above five seconds. Every one was a
first-touch multiway solve at a depth production prewarms
(`MULTIWAY_PREWARM_STACK_DEPTHS` is exactly the 100/50/20bb this
benchmark uses). **That figure would have been reported as a serious
user-facing latency defect and it is an artifact of the instrument.**

Fixed by `bench/server_warmup.py`. Warming costs 3.5 minutes
(3-max 22–28s per depth, 6-max 44–53s), which is the price of measuring
the product a user meets rather than the one a cold process presents.

The old harness carried its own copy of this warm-up with a comment
explaining the trap. It did not carry over because it lived in a script
rather than a module — which is the whole argument for `bench/`.

## What is healthy

- **Zero correctness defects across 1,730 decisions.** No unaffordable
  advice, no malformed distribution, no premium folded preflop, no
  uniform row served at high confidence.
- **1,729 of 1,730 decisions inside the 5-second bar**, p50 under 0.8s
  on both arms.
- **The disclosure machinery works.** Where a signal is meant to fire it
  fires: M251's warning appeared on real decisions within hours of
  shipping, and the F41/F43 signals fired on the rare nodes they exist
  for (0.23% each) rather than never.
- **The refusal rate halved** with a generator that builds legal paths,
  from 6.4% to 1.5–3.0%.

## Recommendations

| # | finding | action | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | F51 | expose fired advisory notes as a structured list | **executed** |
| R2 | F52 | measure the 0–1 raise two-live preflop cell | **unresolved (M253)** |
| R3 | F53 | re-measure the flop tail in reference units | **answered (M253)** |

**R2 (M253)** ran and could not settle it. Pricing the cell by how wide
an opponent must be for a call to break even gives 1 of 80 shallow rows
wrong — leave the gate — but that assumes a weak hand realises its raw
equity, and the verdict flips at a realisation factor of ~0.87, inside
the plausible 0.80–0.90 band. The gate is unchanged for want of
evidence, not because it was shown right. M251's deep cell is unaffected
(82–103 wrong of 128 at every factor).

**R3 (M253)** answered, and the question was posed wrong. Split by live
count, M252's own flop latency climbs from four players up, and six-live
flops were **1 of 181 and 1 of 161** decisions — the breach was the
worst shape, sampled twice. Measured directly on 40 cold six-live flops:
p50 **7.429s / 11.413 reference units**, 38 of 40 over five seconds,
with the machine drifting **2.65x** inside the run. Five seconds is
18.44 units at that run's fastest, so the same work is ~3.1s on a still
machine and ~7.4s on this one. **The cost is what a six-way flop costs;
whether it clears a wall-clock bar is a property of the machine.** Cost
scales with live count and nothing caps total work — only per-seat width
— which is the shape of a fix, unmeasured, at ~0.6% exposure.
| R4 | F54 | move benchmark instruments into `bench/` with tests | **executed** |

**Explicitly not recommended:** anything aimed at the 20% exposure to
multiway postflop irreproducibility. M245 measured width (inert),
iterations (0.240 at 150x the budget) and ensembles (worst cases
untouched at every K). It is disclosed because it cannot currently be
fixed, and a fifth attempt at a configuration would be the mistake M232
named for the turn.
